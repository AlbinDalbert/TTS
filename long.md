Beneath the Whispering Sands


An original Dune fan fiction scene

The wind was keening tonight—an almost human sound as it scraped across the rocks of Sietch Tabr. Its voice carried grains of sand that hissed along the walls and rattled the tent flaps; to others it might have been maddening, but to me, it whispered memory.

Paul slept only a few steps away, though even in his rest I could feel the weight of his new awareness pressing out to fill the space between us. He was changing again—faster, deeper, pulled by forces that even I, trained as I am, dare not name aloud. My heart, Bene Gesserit though it is, betrayed its rhythm every time I thought of what he might become.

I knelt, drawing water from the stilltent’s catch basin, the faint taste of plastic and metal strong in the air. Water is life. The words, once foreign, now held a truth so complete it left no room for irony.

Through the slit of the tent I could see the dunes glowing faintly under the twin moons. For a moment I thought I saw movement—perhaps a small ripple that might have been the shifting of a worm, or perhaps merely wind. Everything on Arrakis blurs the line between life and illusion.

I pressed my fingers together, testing my control. Breath steady, mind clear, body still. “Fear is the little death,” I whispered, not in litany but as acknowledgment. Fear would come, and I would master it—again and again, until the desert itself learned my name.

And yet… when I looked at Paul, I wondered if the Sisterhood had ever truly understood what they unleashed in him—what we had unleashed together.

“There is prophecy,” I murmured to the dark. “But prophecy bends before choice.”

Outside, the dunes sighed in answer. Or perhaps it was the desert deciding whether to accept me, Lady Jessica of Caladan, mother of Muad’Dib—or to consume me whole.
